I am an undergraduate student in Mathematics at the University of Warwick. My main research direction is machine learning theory, with a current focus on neural-network parameterization, training dynamics, and hyperparameter transfer under width scaling.

My current project studies learning-rate transfer under µP: whether the optimal learning rate stabilises at a non-zero limit as network width grows, and under which mathematical conditions such a statement is valid. I mainly use asymptotic analysis, Tensor Programs, and polynomial methods.

I also plan to study high-dimensional PCA, spiked covariance models, and random-matrix phase transitions, while continuing to work on stabilizer formalism, Pauli operators, Clifford operations, and symplectic linear algebra in quantum computing.

Background and Current Work

Summer research project · Supervisor: Professor Fanghui Liu

Background

Maximal Update Parametrization (µP) is designed to preserve non-trivial feature learning as neural-network width increases. One of its most important empirical properties is hyperparameter transfer: a learning rate tuned on a smaller model may remain effective for substantially wider models. The original paper studies this question for deep linear MLPs, expresses the fixed-step network output as a polynomial in the learning rate, and uses Tensor Programs to analyse the infinite-width limits of the loss and its minimizers.

My Work

My work provides a rigorous audit and completion of the general fixed-step argument, and clarifies the precise scope of the theorem. Rather than merely restating the proof, I examine whether the polynomial structure, Tensor Program representation, coefficient convergence, compact-uniform convergence, and argmin stability are sufficient to support each claimed conclusion.

Current Findings
  • Established a width-independent polynomial degree bound for the full training trajectory.
  • Constructed an exact full-batch gradient-descent Tensor Program and verified the Master Theorem hypotheses one by one.
  • Used finite evaluations and Vandermonde inversion to prove coefficient convergence, yielding compact-uniform convergence of outputs, losses, and fixed derivatives.
  • Corrected the compact argmin theorem and completed a rigorous fixed-step compact-interval learning-rate-transfer theorem under uniqueness.
  • Constructed an exact in-model counterexample showing that arbitrary global-minimizer selections need not transfer.
  • Showed that limiting minimizers need not be unique and that general-step rates require additional quantitative estimates.

Theoretical Status

Compact interval Valid under explicit conditions

Fixed training time, a prescribed compact interval, and a unique limiting minimizer inside that interval.

Global argmin False for arbitrary selections

Finite-width models can contain global minimizers escaping to infinity, so the full argmin family is not uniformly bounded.

Uniqueness & rates Require further assumptions

The limiting optimum need not be unique, and the qualitative Tensor Program limit alone gives no general convergence rate.
