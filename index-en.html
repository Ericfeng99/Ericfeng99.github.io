About

I am an undergraduate student in Mathematics at the University of Warwick, with academic interests in the mathematical theory of machine learning, infinite-width neural networks, neural-network parameterizations, training dynamics, and linear-algebraic structures in quantum computing.

My current work focuses on learning-rate transfer under the Maximal Update Parametrization (µP): whether the optimal learning rate converges to a non-zero constant as network width grows, allowing learning rates tuned on smaller models to transfer to wider models without large-scale retuning. Starting from linear multilayer perceptrons, I study wide-width limits, feature learning, and the dependence of training loss on the learning rate under µP, Standard Parametrization (SP), and Neural Tangent Parametrization (NTP).

I am also interested in stabilizer formalism, Pauli operators, Clifford operations, and symplectic linear-algebraic methods in quantum computing.

This website collects selected essays, projects, notes, and academic materials.

Projects

Learning Rate Transfer under µP

Summer research project / neural-network parameterizations, wide-width limits, and learning-rate transfer

Under the supervision of Professor Fanghui Liu, my current summer research project is centred on A Proof of Learning Rate Transfer under µP. It studies learning-rate transfer under the Maximal Update Parametrization: whether the optimal learning rate converges to a non-zero limit as network width grows, and how this supports tuning hyperparameters on smaller models before transferring them to wider ones. Starting from the training dynamics of linear multilayer perceptrons, the project considers the polynomial dependence of training loss on the learning rate, feature learning under µP, and the contrast between µP, Standard Parametrization, and Neural Tangent Parametrization.

Third-Year Essay Plan: Phase Transitions in High-Dimensional PCA

Planned third-year essay / random matrix theory and high-dimensional statistics

A planned essay on high-dimensional PCA under the spiked covariance model, with emphasis on BBP-type phase transitions, alignment between sample principal components and the true signal direction, and the model's relevance to modern machine learning theory.
